Every time your thermostat calls for heat, the furnace runs a fixed checklist. The inducer motor starts and proves draft by closing a pressure switch, the hot surface igniter heats up, the gas valve opens, the burners light, and the flame sensor confirms there is fire. Only after all of that does the blower begin sending warm air into the house. Finding the step that fails is most of the job. Around Racine, WI, the usual failure points are a flame sensor fouled with oxide, an igniter cracked by years of heating and cooling, a pressure switch that stays open because of a weak inducer or a clogged condensate trap on a high-efficiency unit, and control boards with burned relays. Any one of them halts the startup, and from the hallway they all look alike.

Racine HVAC repairs furnaces by testing the sequence, not guessing at it. Our experienced technicians verify each stage with a meter rather than a hunch: igniter resistance, flame signal in microamps, pressure switch operation against actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor health. Every visit includes the safety fundamentals on a fuel-burning appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak-testing of gas connections, and flue draft verification, because the difference between a furnace problem and a furnace hazard is not visible from the thermostat. We measure temperature rise against the data plate before we leave so the fire and the airflow are matched the way the manufacturer intended.

We work on gas, propane, and electric models from every major brand, both conventional 80 percent units and condensing high-efficiency models with their traps, drains, and dual pressure switches. The truck stock covers the parts that actually fail: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors, so most no-heat calls end with heat the same visit. When to Call for Furnace Repair in Racine

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. These are the signals our technicians see most often when a problem is underway:

  • Burners light, run a few seconds, then shut down repeatedly
  • Water, rust stains, or black soot around the flue or cabinet
  • Loud booms at startup caused by delayed ignition
  • A fan that keeps running while the registers blow cool air
  • Squealing or grinding from the blower compartment
  • Frequent on-off cycling that never reaches the thermostat setting
  • The igniter glows and clicks, yet no flame ever appears
  • Yellow, wavering burner flames where they should be sharp blue

Why Professional Furnace Repair Matters

Gas, open flame, high voltage, and a flue all share one cabinet in a furnace, and the most dangerous failure is the one you cannot see: a damaged heat exchanger that lets combustion gases into the air the blower circulates. Every morning that delayed ignition keeps booming at startup, that exchanger takes more stress. The diagnostic side has its own pitfalls. When a furnace trips its high limit, the switch itself may be bad, or the furnace may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a failing blower. Swapping the switch in that second case removes a safety device and leaves the actual problem in place.

At Racine HVAC, no symptom gets treated in isolation. A meter tells us the flame signal, so we never condemn a good sensor while the real culprit, a cracked igniter or a weak gas valve, stays in place. We confirm draft at both the pressure switch and the flue, measure temperature rise against the data plate to flag airflow problems early, and leak-test every gas connection with instruments, not a guess. When we are done, the furnace has proven itself safe and correct through a full cycle, and if it is nearing the end of its useful life, we tell you honestly.

Furnace Repair Prevention Tips

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Book a fall tune-up so weak igniters and fouled sensors are caught before the first freeze
  • Pay attention when the burners light, since a boom instead of a soft whoosh calls for service
  • If you have a high-efficiency furnace, check that the condensate drain is flowing freely
  • Keep a working carbon monoxide alarm on every level of the home and check it each month
  • Keep storage, lint, and especially chlorine-based products away from the furnace closet
  • Make sure the furnace has clear combustion air; a packed utility room starves the burners
  • Put in a fresh filter monthly through the heating season, the cheapest protection your furnace can get
